1. Cyber incidents

Rosstat (Federal State Statistics Service of Russia)

  • Industry: Government
  • Threat actor: Belarusian Cyber Partisans and Silent Crow
  • Date reported: Nov 7–8, 2026 (attacks Oct 29–Nov 1)
  • Status: Confirmed (agency acknowledges the cyberattacks; denies a data leak)
  • Summary: Belarusian Cyber Partisans and the pro-Ukraine group Silent Crow say they spent months inside Rosstat and then launched a destructive attack overnight Oct 31–Nov 1, later publishing death-record analysis they say shows about 393,881 to 450,100 Russia-linked war deaths since February 2022. Those casualty figures have not been independently verified, and the groups’ claims about how much data was taken vary by report. Rosstat confirmed it was targeted in cyberattacks between Oct 29 and Nov 1 but says it found no evidence that databases were compromised or information leaked.
  • Supply chain / third-party risk: Medium for anyone who depends on official Russian statistical feeds or shared demographic systems—if the leak claim is accurate, census, mortality, and related personal records could be exposed beyond the war-casualty figures.
  • Source(s): Kyiv Independent, Euronews, The Record

Italian Foreign Ministry / Antonio Tajani

  • Industry: Government
  • Threat actor: Unknown (Italian officials blame pro-Russian hackers; some local press named NoName057(16), not independently confirmed)
  • Date reported: Nov 7, 2026
  • Status: Confirmed
  • Summary: Italian Foreign Minister and Deputy Prime Minister Antonio Tajani said Saturday that cybercriminals had been publishing his personal data on the dark web for days and were seeking more information about him and his family, including offers to pay for details. He tied the targeting to Italy’s support for Ukraine and asked that hybrid threats be discussed at the Nov 9–10 G7 foreign ministers’ meeting in Paris. Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni blamed pro-Russian hackers and called the sale of officials’ personal data a serious concern; the foreign ministry said other officials, including Rome’s chief prosecutor, received similar threats.
  • Supply chain / third-party risk: Low for commercial supply chains—this is a targeted leak and intimidation campaign against government officials rather than a vendor or B2B network breach.
  • Source(s): AP News, BBC, Italian Foreign Ministry
