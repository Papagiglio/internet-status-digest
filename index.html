1. Cyber incidents

U.S. Department of Homeland Security (DHS, CBP, ICE)

  • Industry: Government
  • Threat actor: Hacktivists ("Icepick" leak, published by Distributed Denial of Secrets)
  • Date reported: Oct 28, 2026
  • Status: Investigating
  • Summary: Distributed Denial of Secrets published names, phone numbers and assignments of about 40,000 DHS, CBP and ICE employees, reportedly pulled from a DHS Okta single sign-on directory. WIRED verified parts of the data, and Okta denies its own systems were compromised.
  • Supply chain / third-party risk: Medium. If the data came from a misconfigured or abused identity directory, other agencies and contractors using the same setup could be exposed, and the leaked staff details invite targeted phishing.
  • Source: X news summary

Unnamed California city (police and 911 data)

  • Industry: Government
  • Threat actor: Rhysida
  • Date reported: Oct 28, 2026
  • Status: Claimed
  • Summary: Rhysida posted a leak-site listing claiming police and 911 data from a California city it didn't name. The claim hasn't been verified.
  • Supply chain / third-party risk: Low for business, but it's a public-safety system.
  • Source: RansomNews

EWM Group (Peacocks, Bonmarché)

  • Industry: Retail
  • Threat actor: Aurora
  • Date reported: Oct 28, 2026
  • Status: Claimed
  • Summary: The Aurora group listed the UK fashion retailer EWM Group, which owns Peacocks and Bonmarché, on its leak site. Neither the scope nor the claim has been confirmed.
  • Supply chain / third-party risk: Medium for its suppliers and logistics partners if order or partner data was taken.
  • Source: RansomNews

Chrome Web Store search-hijacking campaign

  • Industry: Technology providers
  • Threat actor: Unknown
  • Date reported: Oct 29, 2026
  • Status: Confirmed
  • Summary: Researchers found 319 Chrome extensions, with about 13.4 million combined installs, quietly sending users' searches through tracking servers to make advertising money from Bing and Yahoo.
  • Supply chain / third-party risk: Medium. Unvetted browser extensions on work PCs can see and redirect browsing traffic, so it's worth auditing what's installed.
  • Source: BleepingComputer

Ledger hardware wallets sold in Singapore

  • Industry: Technology / retail (hardware supply chain)
  • Threat actor: Unknown
  • Date reported: Oct 29, 2026
  • Status: Investigating
  • Summary: Singapore police say tampered Ledger hardware wallets sold through local retailers stole recovery phrases, costing two users about $2.7 million. Ledger has asked resellers in the region to stop sales while it reviews.
  • Supply chain / third-party risk: High for hardware bought through third-party resellers, since the devices were compromised before they reached buyers.
  • Source: X news summary

Morrison Foerster

  • Industry: Legal services
  • Threat actor: SilentRansomGroup
  • Date reported: Oct 28, 2026
  • Status: Claimed
  • Summary: SilentRansomGroup listed the US law firm Morrison Foerster on its leak site. The firm hasn't confirmed an incident.
  • Supply chain / third-party risk: Medium, because large law firms hold confidential deal and litigation files for many corporate clients.
  • Source: RansomNews

zkLink Nova bridge

  • Industry: Crypto / fintech
  • Threat actor: Unknown
  • Date reported: Oct 29, 2026
  • Status: Investigating
  • Summary: An attacker drained about $786,000 from the zkLink Nova Ethereum bridge late Oct 28 by reusing a valid message hash to forge withdrawals. zkLink paused the affected contracts and offered a 20% bounty.
  • Supply chain / third-party risk: Low outside crypto.
  • Source: X news summary

Virtualine bulletproof hosting (law enforcement action)

  • Industry: Cybercrime infrastructure
  • Threat actor: Virtualine (hosting used by ransomware and other criminals)
  • Date reported: Oct 28, 2026
  • Status: Resolved (domain seized)
  • Summary: U.S. law enforcement seized the main domain of Virtualine, a hosting service advertised on crime forums for ransomware and other illegal activity.
  • Supply chain / third-party risk: Positive in the short term, though such groups usually move to new hosting quickly.
  • Source: BleepingComputer

2. AI service outages

  • Grok Bot, Cloud Agents and Origin were degraded from about 4:15 PM CDT Oct 28 for roughly two hours, leaving many bots unresponsive before service recovered. Status: resolved. X news summary

3. Internet, network, cloud and EDI VAN issues

  • A deep-sea fiber cut near Mtunzini, South Africa damaged the Seacom and EASSy submarine cables, slowing Safaricom home fiber and other Kenyan internet providers while traffic shifts to backup cables. Status: ongoing (repairs expected to take about a week). X news summary
  • Bet365 customers in several countries couldn't log in or saw missing bets for about two hours during Oct 28 evening football matches. Status: resolved. X news summary
  • AWS, Google Cloud, Microsoft Azure (no active events) and Oracle Cloud (Normal Performance) had no new incidents, and Cloudflare had only a few short, minor incidents that are all resolved. The EDI VAN pages I could read show nothing new: Cleo, SPS Commerce and TrueCommerce are all operational, and OpenText lists only an old Middle East item from May. I couldn't read the DiCentral, Descartes or ECGrid pages.
