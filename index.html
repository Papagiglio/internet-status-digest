1. Cyber incidents

Beget (Russia)

  • Industry: Technology providers / web hosting, cloud and domain registrar
  • Threat actor: Unknown (no claim)
  • Date reported: Nov 3–4, 2026
  • Status: Confirmed (recovery under way)
  • Summary: Overnight Nov 1–2, attackers reached Beget’s infrastructure-management systems and ran wiper scripts that erased VPS, S3 and Kubernetes data plus all binary VPS backups; file-level backups survived. More than 1,000 servers must be checked by hand, and some sites are back while most shared hosting is expected to recover. Disruption hit businesses, government sites and Russia’s Chestny Znak product-labeling system.
  • Supply chain / third-party risk: High for anyone hosted there or depending on services that are, including retailers and manufacturers tied to Chestny Znak labeling; rotate any credentials stored on Beget servers.
  • Source: The Record, Kommersant

DigiCert

  • Industry: Technology providers / certificate authority
  • Threat actor: Unknown
  • Date reported: Nov 1–5, 2026 (full incident report Nov 1, wide coverage Nov 5)
  • Status: Confirmed
  • Summary: An internal archive of customer validation records was left reachable from the internet after a February 2025 migration, and a self-registered account without MFA read part of it from June 27 to July 2, 2026. Most of the data was business contact details, but a small minority of accounts included passports, driver’s licenses or utility bills. DigiCert says CA systems, CertCentral, DigiCert ONE, logins and certificates were not affected, and no certificates need replacing; it hasn’t said how many people were hit.
  • Supply chain / third-party risk: Medium. Exposed contact and ID data on certificate admins is useful for targeted phishing and impersonation against companies that buy certificates.
  • Source: Cybersecurity News, Zyphe summary of DigiCert’s report

Zyxel GS1900 network switches

  • Industry: Technology providers / network equipment
  • Threat actor: Kapibala
  • Date reported: Nov 4–5, 2026
  • Status: Confirmed (exploited Aug–Sep 2026; fixed firmware available)
  • Summary: Switch hacks once blamed on a June flaw actually used a separate zero-day, CVE-2026-107441, that bypassed the June patch and let attackers run commands as root; nearly 1,000 switches in 48 countries were compromised. Zyxel’s fix is firmware 2.90 Patch 3 (advisory Nov 3).
  • Supply chain / third-party risk: Medium–high for small offices, plants and branch sites running GS1900 switches; patched-in-June devices were still vulnerable, so update and check for compromise.
  • Source: BleepingComputer, Zyxel advisory

Apple devices (CVE-2024-27834 added to CISA’s exploited list)

  • Industry: Technology providers
  • Threat actor: Unknown (info-stealer operators per CISA coverage)
  • Date reported: Nov 4, 2026
  • Status: Confirmed (patched in 2024)
  • Summary: CISA added an old, already-patched Apple WebKit flaw to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog after it was used to deliver info-stealing malware. Devices still running unpatched older Apple OS versions are the ones at risk.
  • Supply chain / third-party risk: Low–medium; mainly a patch-hygiene check for older Macs and iPhones in the fleet.
  • Source: The Hacker News, CISA KEV

Linux servers running Control Web Panel (CWPdoor backdoor)

  • Industry: Technology providers / web hosting
  • Threat actor: Unknown (sample found alongside the Ebury backdoor)
  • Date reported: Nov 4–5, 2026
  • Status: Confirmed (real victims in the wild)
  • Summary: XLab found a trojanized build of cwpsrv, the web server behind the CWP admin panel, that sits dormant until it gets a signed, encrypted HTTP request, then runs attacker commands and hides the request from logs. It replaces versions 1.15.10 and 1.24.0 and had zero antivirus detections; more than 340,000 CWP panels are internet-facing.
  • Supply chain / third-party risk: Medium for hosting providers and businesses using CWP-managed servers; check for the ngx_http_integrity_helper socket and rotate panel credentials if infected.
  • Source: BleepingComputer, XLab

SignalPlus

  • Industry: Financial services / crypto trading tools
  • Threat actor: Unknown
  • Date reported: Nov 4–5, 2026
  • Status: Confirmed (services suspended)
  • Summary: SignalPlus says a compromised third-party Telegram account and a fake Teams invite led to an attack with under $82,000 in losses, which it will reimburse. Binance and Deribit revoked every SignalPlus-linked API key and say their own systems and funds weren’t affected.
  • Supply chain / third-party risk: Medium for traders who connected SignalPlus to other exchanges; revoke those keys.
  • Source: CoinDesk, PANews

Queens Public Library (New York)

  • Industry: Government / public library
  • Threat actor: Unknown
  • Date reported: Nov 5, 2026 (attack Nov 3)
  • Status: Investigating (services still down)
  • Summary: Ransomware knocked out the library’s website, app, public computers, Wi-Fi, self-checkout and digital media. All 66 branches stayed open, and the library is working with law enforcement on a recovery it says will take time.
  • Supply chain / third-party risk: Low for businesses; possible patron-data exposure is not yet known.
  • Source: The Register, Gothamist
