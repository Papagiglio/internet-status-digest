1. Cyber incidents

Crédit Mutuel Arkéa (France)

  • Industry: Financial services / banking
  • Threat actor: Unknown
  • Date reported: Nov 4, 2026
  • Status: Confirmed (contained; recovery under way)
  • Summary: The French regional bank confirmed a cyberattack that let attackers use the bank’s own account to send fraudulent transfers to other banks, then likely into crypto. Entry was through a supplier platform, not Fortuneo. Clients’ funds were not hit; some transfers were delayed while Arkéa paused activity. It did not confirm ZachXBT’s ~$133M / €118M figure but said losses are in that order of magnitude and not a threat to the bank’s soundness; some funds were blocked and recovery is ongoing.
  • Supply chain / third-party risk: High for banks and payment providers that trust supplier platforms for treasury or settlement access; a vendor compromise can move institutional funds without touching retail accounts.
  • Source: Crédit Mutuel Arkéa, AFP

Managed service providers and their Microsoft 365 / Azure clients (Todyl “Key Eater” campaign)

  • Industry: Technology providers / MSPs (downstream: government, energy, food & agriculture, transportation, education, legal, nonprofits)
  • Threat actor: Unknown
  • Date reported: Nov 3–4, 2026
  • Status: Ongoing
  • Summary: Todyl says that since Nov 1 one operator has been signing in as MSPs’ own Entra ID apps with stolen, still-valid secrets (and some Azure DevOps federated identities / session tokens), reaching about 45 app registrations across ~18 orgs and 27 organizations so far. App sign-ins skip MFA and user Conditional Access; the actor reads Key Vault, bulk-downloads credential and config files from SharePoint, and fans out through MSP multi-tenant / GDAP access. All confirmed activity came from 199.19.77.84; the latest attempt Todyl saw was blocked. No Microsoft vulnerability is involved.
  • Supply chain / third-party risk: Very high. One compromised MSP app can expose every client it manages; harvested secrets target VPNs, RMM, backups, firewalls, and AI keys.
  • Source: Todyl

Organizations hit by APT29 watering-hole campaign (incl. U.S. chemical-industry site and Plerdy SEO script)

  • Industry: Government / espionage targets (also commercial sites loading the compromised tracker)
  • Threat actor: APT29 (Midnight Blizzard), Russia-aligned per Sekoia
  • Date reported: Nov 4, 2026 (campaign window Aug 27–Sep 3 called out in coverage)
  • Status: Confirmed (patches available; campaign described as past window)
  • Summary: Sekoia attributes a watering-hole campaign that profiled visitors then delivered zero-click N-day exploit chains for already-patched iOS and Android/Chrome flaws, stealing cookies, messaging databases, Keychain data, location and photos. Compromised sites included a U.S. chemical industry trade association and a Ukrainian web-analytics script (Plerdy) loaded by thousands of sites. Apple and Google have patched the flaws used; updating devices is the fix. Malware does not survive a reboot.
  • Supply chain / third-party risk: Medium–high for anyone loading third-party analytics/SEO scripts, and for mobile fleets that lag on OS patches.
  • Source: Sekoia, BleepingComputer, The Hacker News

Google Workspace users targeted by Calendar-invite session phishing (“Operation CloudFire”)

  • Industry: Technology / enterprise productivity
  • Threat actor: Unknown
  • Date reported: Nov 4, 2026 (active since mid-August)
  • Status: Investigating (Material says the Google wave looks dispersed; dormant Microsoft 365 variant exists)
  • Summary: Material Security documented a campaign that sent Calendar invites from compromised, familiar senders, routed victims through Google’s own redirector to a reverse-proxied Google sign-in that relays MFA codes and number matching, capturing live sessions past MFA. Material took down 897 domains and blocked tens of thousands of messages; themes have shifted toward Drive share invites, and an unfinished Microsoft 365 branch was found but not activated.
  • Supply chain / third-party risk: Medium. Hijacked Workspace sessions reach mail, Drive, and OAuth grants in the victim’s name; review audit logs and revoke sessions quickly.
  • Source: BleepingComputer

NIKKE / Level Infinite (Tencent) PC launcher

  • Industry: Technology / gaming
  • Threat actor: Unknown
  • Date reported: Nov 4, 2026
  • Status: Confirmed (investigating impact)
  • Summary: Level Infinite confirmed that NIKKE PC launcher script files may have been tampered with; it patched the client around 11:30 AM CST Nov 3 and is assessing damage. Reporting says CDN JavaScript altered since late September put PC-launcher users at risk of a remote-access trojan that can steal browser passwords, tokens, API keys and crypto-wallet data. Mobile versions are not implicated; already-infected PCs need cleanup beyond the patch.
  • Supply chain / third-party risk: Medium for players and for any shared workstations that ran the launcher; rotate passwords, tokens, and wallet keys if the PC was used.
  • Source: Asiae / Level Infinite confirmation via Scout

Kyiv Independent

  • Industry: Media / news
  • Threat actor: Unknown (Russia suspected, not confirmed)
  • Date reported: Nov 4, 2026
  • Status: Investigating (unauthorized posts removed; donor/payment data reportedly not accessed)
  • Summary: Attackers gained access to the outlet’s CMS and a newsletter service on Nov 4 and sent false Russian- and English-language emails, including claims about a nuclear warhead aimed at Kyiv and fabricated Zelensky/Putin messaging. Staff deleted unauthorized site posts, told readers their banking/donor details appear untouched so far, and say the content points to a Russian operation.
  • Supply chain / third-party risk: Low for most businesses; high reputational/info-ops impact for anyone amplifying unverified crisis emails.
  • Source: Kyiv Independent
